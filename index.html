      How it's calculated

      SGPA for one semester

      Every course earns credit points: its credits multiplied by the grade point of the grade you got. The SGPA is the total credit points divided by the total credits.

      SGPA = Σ(Ci × Gi) / ΣCi

      Worked example

      This is the illustration printed in the JNTUH R22 academic regulations:

      Course Credits Grade Points Credit points
      Course 1 4 A 8 32
      Course 2 4 O 10 40
      Course 3 4 C 5 20
      Course 4 3 B 6 18
      Course 5 3 A+ 9 27
      Course 6 3 C 5 15
      Total 21 152

      SGPA = 152 / 21 = 7.238… which is printed as 7.24.

      CGPA across semesters

      The CGPA applies the same formula to every course you have taken so far. It is not the average of your SGPAs, because a 24-credit semester should count for more than a 16-credit one.

      Semester 1: 20 credits at 9.00. Semester 2: 10 credits at 6.00. Averaging the two SGPAs gives 7.50, but the real CGPA is (180 + 60) / 30 = 8.00.

      Failed courses and pending results

      An F or Ab keeps its credits in the denominator with zero points, so it pulls the SGPA down until it is cleared. Anna University is the exception: R2021 leaves U grades out of the GPA altogether. Courses without a grade are skipped, so you can enter a semester before its results are out.

      Rounding

      Everything is kept at full precision and rounded to two decimals only when shown, the way grade sheets print it. The CGPA is built from total credit points, never from rounded SGPAs. Percentage and class use the rounded CGPA, and the planner rounds the SGPA you need up, so the number it shows is always enough.
